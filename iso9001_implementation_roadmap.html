ISO 9001 Implementation Roadmap — six phases from gap analysis through certification

Typical timeline: 9–18 months for first-time certification. Smaller orgs can do it in 6–9 months.

1
Gap analysis — Weeks 1–4
Where are you now vs. what ISO 9001:2015 requires?
Download free ISO 9001:2015 gap checklist (BSI or ASQ)
Define your organization's scope — which sites, products, services
Interview dept. heads on current processes & documentation
Rate each of the 10 ISO clauses: Compliant / Partial / Gap
Key output: A scored gap report ranking your biggest risk areas. This drives your whole implementation plan.
2
Planning & leadership buy-in — Weeks 4–8
ISO 9001 requires top management commitment — this is non-negotiable
Build a project plan with milestones, owners, target audit date
Appoint a QMS Management Representative (could be you)
Define quality policy and measurable quality objectives
Select a certification body (registrar) and get a quote
Free resource: ISO's free "Moving from ISO 9001:2008 to ISO 9001:2015" transition guide explains all clause changes clearly — great onboarding doc for leadership.
3
QMS documentation & process design — Months 2–7
The bulk of the work — build your documented system
Write the Quality Manual (scope, policy, process overview)
Document core process procedures (SOPs) for each major function
Create mandatory records: NCR forms, corrective action, audit logs
Build a risk register (Clause 6.1 — risk-based thinking)
Establish supplier/vendor evaluation procedure
Define KPIs for each quality objective
Key output: A document control system (even a simple SharePoint or Google Drive folder structure works) with version-controlled docs, a process map, and completed mandatory records.
4
Implementation & training — Months 5–9
Put the documented system into actual daily practice
Train all staff on QMS procedures relevant to their role
Run processes under the new QMS for at least 3 months
Log nonconformances (NCRs) and corrective actions as they occur
Collect KPI data against your quality objectives
Note: Auditors look for evidence the system is actually running — NCR records, meeting minutes, corrective actions closed out. Start collecting this from day one of implementation.
5
Internal audit & management review — Month 8–10
Required by the standard before the external audit
Conduct internal audit across all clauses and processes
Write internal audit report with findings
Hold formal Management Review meeting — document minutes
Close all major nonconformances before Stage 2 audit
Tip: Your internal auditor cannot audit their own work. Train a second person or hire a freelance auditor ($500–1,500) for objectivity and to simulate the real audit experience.
6
Certification audit — Months 10–12+
Two-stage external audit by your chosen registrar
Stage 1: Document review — auditor checks your QMS docs are compliant
Stage 2: On-site audit — evidence that the system is actually running
Respond to any findings within agreed timeframe
Receive certificate (valid 3 years, annual surveillance audits)
After certification: You'll have annual surveillance audits in years 1 and 2, then a full recertification audit in year 3. Keep your NCR log and KPIs running continuously.
