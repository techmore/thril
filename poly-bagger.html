Startup checklist
0/12
Startup complete — ready for production.
Do not operate without compressed air. Running the T-1000 below 60 PSI will cause incomplete seals, jammed film, and potential damage to the sealing mechanism. Always verify the pressure gauge before starting production.
Disengage lever (front-left) & "Bagger Error": The lever on the front-left of the machine opens the seal frame. If it is left open, the display shows Bagger Error on a red screen with a Seal Frame Open note. Close the lever fully and the machine clears the error before restarting the cycle.
Label issues? If a label is crooked, upside down, or won't print correctly, do not continue — stop and seek assistance from the bagging area lead. See the Rebagging procedure for handling already-bagged product.

Compressor setup

The T-1000 runs on compressed air. Correct compressor configuration protects the sealer and keeps seal quality consistent. Set up the air supply before every startup.

Required air supply

ParameterSetting
Operating pressure60 PSI at the machine inlet
Minimum safe pressureKeep at 60 PSI — do not run below it
RegulatorCompressor side, set to 60 PSI
Ball valveClosed before starting compressor, then open during operation, closed at shutdown
Hose connectionQuick-disconnect on the T-1000 rear panel

Compressor startup sequence

  1. Drain the tank — open the compressor drain valve until water stops flowing, then close it
  2. Close the ball valve — make sure the ball valve on the compressor outlet is closed before starting the compressor
  3. Connect the air hose — attach to the T-1000 quick-disconnect on the rear panel; listen for the coupling to seat. Do this before starting the compressor while it is easier to connect
  4. Start the compressor — with the ball valve still closed, start the compressor and let it build pressure
  5. Open the ball valve — once the compressor has built pressure, open the ball valve on the compressor outlet
  6. Set the regulator — adjust to 60 PSI at the compressor side
  7. Verify pressure — check the T-1000 gauge reads 60 PSI before starting production
  8. Check the water separator / air catch — drain any accumulated moisture from the filter bowl or air catch

Daily compressor checks

CheckFrequency
Drain water from the compressor tankStart of every shift
Verify regulator reads 60 PSIEvery startup
Inspect air hose for cracks or loose fittingsDaily
Drain the water separator bowlDaily, or when moisture is visible
Run a test seal and inspect qualityEvery startup
Cold startup: Compressors build more water in cold weather. If the machine has been idle overnight, always drain the tank first — moisture in the line drops effective pressure and weakens seals.

Summer water buildup & clogged drain

In warm, humid weather (worst in summer) the tank collects water fast, and debris + sludge can clog the drain valve so it stops draining even with water still inside. This is a known issue on this compressor — here is the recovery that works:

Drain valve jammed / won't drain:
  1. Close the drain valve so the tank can re-pressurize
  2. Close the ball valve to the machine (keep the air out of the line while you work on the drain)
  3. Turn the compressor ON and let it build up pressure with the vent/drain still closed
  4. Open the drain valve once pressure is up — the stored pressure blows the water and debris out in a rush and clears the clog
  5. Repeat if the first blast doesn't fully clear it; then close the drain valve and return to the normal startup sequence

If pressure-blowout stops working (the clog returns sooner each time), the tank sludge has built up past what flushing clears — the drain valve or petcock needs to be removed and cleaned, or the tank professionally flushed.

Production

Change bag & film roll

Change the film roll when the core becomes visible or when switching to a different film gauge. A clean changeover prevents misaligned labels and weak seals.

When to change the film roll

  • Film core becomes visible on the unwind spindle
  • Switching to a different film width or gauge
  • Film is creased, torn, or misfeeding
  • Seal quality degrades on a nearly-empty roll

Changeover steps

  1. Stop the machine — complete the pending cycle, then press E-stop
  2. Open the film guard — release the latch and lift the guard
  3. Remove the empty core — slide it off the unwind spindle and discard
  4. Load the new roll — slide it onto the unwind spindle, matching the film direction shown in the threading diagram inside the guard
  5. Thread the film — route film through the rollers exactly per the diagram; keep tension even and avoid wrinkles
  6. Close the film guard — ensure it seats fully before running
  7. Test cycle — run one test bag, verify seal quality and bag length, then resume production

After every changeover

  • Re-bag any products bagged during the transition (see Rebagging procedure)
  • Confirm the label prints upright and on-center on the first new bag
  • Log the roll change and film type in the production log
Film direction matters. Loading the film backwards or mis-threading a roller is the #1 cause of jams after a changeover. When in doubt, refer to the threading diagram inside the film guard.

Weekly deeper closeout

  • Inspect the film path for debris, dust, or stray film fragments
  • Check the water separator filter for buildup and replace if needed
  • Verify the compressor intake filter is clean
  • Wipe down the control panel and counter display
Extended idle (30+ days): If the machine will sit idle, remove the film roll and store it sealed in a dry area — poly film degrades with heat and light. Operators must recertify before first use after a long idle (see Operator certification).

Label printing

Mac setup & label printing workflow

One-time Mac configuration to connect the Zebra printer, plus the daily workflow for printing UPC barcode labels from .docx source files.

Printer connection (one-time Mac setup)

These steps only need to be done once per Mac workstation:

Connect the Zebra printer

Connect the Zebra ZTC 110Xi4-203dpi printer via USB-B to USB-A cable (or USB-C adapter). The port is on the back of the printer.

Create CUPS print queue

Register the printer queue on macOS:

lpadmin -p Zebra -E \ -v "usb://Zebra%20Technologies/ZTC%20110Xi4-203dpi%20ZPL?serial=JJL100932" \ -o printer-is-shared=false

Verify detection

lpinfo -v | grep -i zebra

Install dependencies

The Python label script requires Pillow for image processing:

pip3 install Pillow
Without -o raw, macOS converts the ZPL to a raster image, producing garbled output. Always pass -o raw when sending ZPL via lp. The one-step script handles this automatically.

One-step label printing

From the poly-bagger/ directory, run print_label.py to extract the image, generate ZPL, and print in one command:

python3 print_label.py "UPC Labels/AKV PLUM - PSA RETAIL.docx"

If the label prints upside down, add --rotate 180:

python3 print_label.py "UPC Labels/AK47 G2 Ribbed PLUM - PSA RETAIL.docx" --rotate 180

Script options

FlagDefaultDescription
--scale4Scale factor: 3 (2.85"), 4 (3.80"), 5 (4.75")
--rotate0Rotate image degrees (180 to fix upside down)
--no-printoffGenerate ZPL only, don't send to printer

Manual workflow

If you need to inspect or modify the intermediate files:

  1. Extract image from .docx: python3 -c "..."
  2. Rotate if needed: python3 -c "Image.open(...).rotate(180).save(...)"
  3. Generate ZPL: encode 1-bit image as ^GFA hex
  4. Send to printer: lp -d Zebra -o raw label.zpl
  5. Verify queue: lpq -P Zebra

Printer queue commands

When to re-bag

SituationAction
Bag torn or punctured during handlingRe-bag with the same label on a fresh bag
Label printed crooked or off-centerRe-bag with a corrected label print
Seal failed (bag not closed)Re-bag; inspect sealer bar for residue or damage
Wrong product in bagRe-bag with correct product and label
QC inspection failureRepair/rework per NCR, then re-bag with original label if product passes
Film roll change caused misaligned printRe-bag any products bagged during the transition

Step-by-step re-bagging

  1. Inspect the product — ensure it isn't damaged and still meets QC criteria
  2. Remove old bag — cut or tear open the original bag; discard it
  3. Verify the label — confirm the existing label is correct and legible, or print a new one
  4. Place product in new bag — align the product so the label faces outward and is readable
  5. Position the label — ensure the barcode is not wrinkled, folded, or obscured
  6. Seal the bag — run through the T-1000 sealer or use a hand sealer if doing a single bag
  7. Verify the seal — tug test on both ends of the seal; no gaps
  8. QC check — confirm the barcode scans, label is upright, bag is clean

Label reuse rules

  1. If the original label is undamaged and still adhered, it can be reused on the new bag
  2. If the label lifted or is creased, print a fresh label using print_label.py
  3. Never hand-write a replacement barcode — always print from the .docx source
  4. Re-bagged items must be logged in the rework log (form REWORK-001)

Troubleshooting: label placement & orientation

Label position on the bag can vary due to printer calibration, media alignment, padding values, or orientation. Use this guide to isolate and correct placement issues.

Placement variance: root causes & fixes

SymptomRoot causeFix
Label shifted too far left left_margin too low Increase left_margin (default 60) in generate_zpl() — try 80–120
Label shifted too far right left_margin too high or ^FO X too large Decrease left_margin (try 40–20) or reduce side_padding
Label too high (near top of bag) top_margin too low Increase top_margin (default 30) — try 50–80
Label too low (near bottom of bag) top_margin too high or bottom_padding too low Decrease top_margin (try 10–20) or increase bottom_padding
Label prints upside down on bag Image not rotated 180° Add --rotate 180 to the script, or rotate the image in Python before ZPL generation
Label prints mirrored / reversed Wrong bit ordering in ^GFA Use byte_val = (byte_val << 1) | bit with bit = 0 if px else 1. First pixel = MSB = leftmost dot. Do not reverse bits.
Bottom of label cut off bottom_padding too low Increase bottom_padding (default 80) — try 120–160
Sides of label cut off side_padding too low Increase side_padding (default 120) — try 160–200
Label prints too large for bag Scale factor too large Reduce --scale from 4 to 3 (2.85" width)
Label prints too small to read Scale factor too small Increase --scale from 4 to 5 (4.75" width)
Printer shows "not ready" / rejects job Bad ZPL with ^PON/^FWN Remove those commands. Reboot printer (power cycle). CUPS re-enable won't fix it.
Garbled / unreadable printout Wrong bit ordering or missing -o raw Check bit ordering in ^GFA. Confirm you're sending with -o raw (or use print_label.py).

Padding values reference

All adjustments are in dots at 203 DPI. Modify these in print_label.py (inside generate_zpl()):

ParameterDefaultEffect
left_margin60Shifts label left/right
top_margin30Shifts label up/down
bottom_padding80Extra space below image (prevents clipping)
side_padding120Total side padding (prevents side clipping)

Mac-specific orientation setup

On macOS, the printer driver and CUPS filters can interfere with raw ZPL passthrough. This setup ensures correct orientation:

  1. Create the print queue with lpadmin as shown above — use the raw USB URI, do not install a macOS printer driver
  2. Always send ZPL with -o raw to bypass macOS rasterization
  3. If the label orientation is wrong, fix it in Python with --rotate 180 — never use ZPL orientation commands
  4. The print_label.py script handles all of this automatically: raw queue, correct bit ordering, rotation in Python
After a bad ZPL job: The printer may show "not ready" persistently. Power-cycle the printer (unplug → wait 10s → plug back in). Do not rely on CUPS commands to clear this state.
Still having issues? Check the compressor pressure (should be 60 PSI), verify the film roll is seated correctly, and ensure the label media matches the printer DPI setting (203 DPI).

Seal defect: root causes & fixes

SymptomRoot causeFix
Machine stalls mid-cycle / jaw motion is weak or slow Air supply starved (compressor behind or drain clogged) Check the T-1000 gauge first: below 60 PSI, drain the compressor tank (see Compressor setup for the clogged-drain blowout recovery), reopen the ball valve, re-verify 60 PSI
Pressure dropped slowly during the run Leaking hose or quick-disconnect, or compressor tripped/off Listen for hiss along the hose and fittings; re-seat the quick-disconnect; confirm the compressor is running and the ball valve is fully open
Seals got weak and machine sounds "wet" Water in the air line (full air catch / separator) Drain the air catch / water separator bowl, then the compressor tank; run 2–3 test bags to purge wet air before resuming production
Weak seal that peels open easily Seal temperature too low Increase seal temp dial in small increments; run test seals until the film fuses
Seal melted through / holes in film Seal temperature too high Decrease seal temp dial; check the film roll label for the recommended temperature
Incomplete seal (partial closure) Low air pressure Verify 60 PSI on the gauge; drain compressor, check hose and ball valve
Seal skips or is uneven along the bar Residue or film contamination on sealing bar Stop machine, clean sealing bar with lint-free cloth once cool
Seal not centered / crooked Film threaded unevenly Re-thread film per diagram, keep tension even and avoid wrinkles
Film sticks to sealing bar Bar too hot or coated with residue Lower temp, clean bar; consider non-stick coating if recurring
Seal quality varies between bags Compressor cycling / pressure fluctuation Verify regulator holds 60 PSI; check for leaks in hose line
Bag won't release from bar Overheated or dirty bar Cool down, clean bar, reduce seal temperature

Seal quality check

Before production and after any temperature, pressure, or film change, verify seals with a tug test: grip both sides of the sealed edge and pull firmly. The seal must hold with no separation or pinholes.

Seal temperature reference

Standard setting used on this machine: 380 °F with a 0.3 second seal dwell time.

Film typeTypical seal temp
Standard LDPE poly (default)380 °F · 0.3 sec
Thin film / lightweightLower than 380 °F
Heavy gauge / thickHigher than 380 °F
Always trust the test, not the dial. Dial positions vary with ambient temperature, film lot, and bar cleanliness. Set the dial near the reference range, then tune with test seals until the tug test passes.
Check air before blaming heat. Low pressure mimics a bad temperature setting (weak, partial seals). Always confirm the T-1000 gauge reads 60 PSI before turning the temp dial — otherwise you chase the wrong cause and risk overheating good film.
Stop and escalate if pressure will not hold at 60 PSI after draining and re-checking, the drain valve stays clogged after the blowout recovery, or you hear continuous hissing with the machine idle. Tag the machine out and call the bagging area lead.
Never ship a bag that fails the tug test. Re-bag the product (see Rebagging procedure) and correct the seal issue before resuming production.
